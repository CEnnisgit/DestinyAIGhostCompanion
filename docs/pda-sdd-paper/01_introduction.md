# §1–2: Introduction & Methodology

## 1. Introduction

Software documentation is a fundamental and indispensable artifact within the ecosystem of software development. It acts as the linchpin for disseminating critical information across a multifaceted spectrum of stakeholders — including architects, programmers, QA engineers, end-users, and maintenance personnel.

Despite its recognized importance, software documentation processes are frequently beset by persistent challenges stemming from:
- The inherent complexity of contemporary software systems
- Significant heterogeneity in stakeholder information requirements
- Limitations in existing models along three principal dimensions: **generality**, **simplicity**, and **efficiency**

### Key Problems with Existing Models

1. **Lack of Generality** — Many models concentrate on discrete facets like APIs or requirements specifications, lacking holistic documentation coverage.
2. **Lack of Simplicity** — Established processes are undermined by requirements for specialized competencies, acting as barriers for stakeholders.
3. **Lack of Efficiency** — Many models demand significant temporal and cognitive resources, leading to incomplete, outdated, or neglected documentation.

This research proposes the **PDA-SDD model** — a holistic, phase-based, and integrated framework ensuring broad applicability for diverse stakeholders throughout the entire software development process.

---

## 2. Methodology and Research Questions

### Research Questions

**RQ1:** What are the essential characteristics required for a comprehensive software documentation model to effectively address the identified challenges of generality, simplicity, and efficiency across diverse software development contexts and stakeholder needs?

**RQ2:** To what extent does the proposed PDA-SDD model exhibit the desired attributes of generality, simplicity, and efficiency, as perceived by software development and documentation experts?

### Approach

- **RQ1** was addressed through comprehensive analysis of existing software documentation models and studies.
- **RQ2** was addressed by developing the theoretical PDA-SDD model, then evaluating it via a digital questionnaire distributed to 150 software development and documentation experts (48% response rate, n = 72).

### Survey Design

- Likert scale: 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree)
- Five main sections: participant info, sub-model evaluations (SRSD, RLD, DDD, CLD, SUMD, EULA), generality statements, simplicity statements, efficiency statements
